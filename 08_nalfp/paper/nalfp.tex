\begin{abstract}
\noindent We propose NALFP, a synthesis of three previously-disconnected
strands of cryptocurrency research: time-varying community detection on
correlation graphs, latent-factor asset pricing, and adaptive portfolio
construction. The novel contribution is to feed two time-varying network
signals --- the within-cluster momentum rank and the cross-cluster relative
strength --- into the characteristic matrix of an Instrumented PCA
\citep{kelly2019characteristics} model as additional instruments alongside the
standard market, momentum, volatility, size, fundamental yield and supply
absorption factors. The IPCA expected returns are then blended with a pure
network signal using an out-of-sample IC-proportional weight, and the
resulting cross-sectional rank drives a weekly-rebalanced long/short quintile
portfolio with explicit cluster-diversification, single-asset, turnover and
volatility-target constraints. On 16 out-of-sample weeks the constrained
strategy delivers a Sharpe of 1.66 (95\% block-bootstrap CI $[-0.71, 7.11]$)
at 5.6\% realized annual volatility and 8.7\% weekly turnover; an
intentionally-uncapped ablation reaches a Sharpe of 3.15 (CI $[1.34, 7.27]$)
on the same window. We document --- and quantify --- where the strategy breaks
down: zero IPCA latent factors clear the $|t|\!\geq\! 1.65$ filter on 24
training weeks, the cluster cap actively reduces Sharpe in this slice of
history, and three of nine instrument signs in $\Gamma$ are inconsistent with
prior crypto factor evidence. The submission is offered as an exercise in
methodological honesty rather than as a claim of identified alpha.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Artemis Analytics competition asks for a systematic, data-driven
rebalancing strategy with two requirements: the factor must be economically
motivated, and the team must be able to articulate the limits of its own
approach \citep{artemis2026competition}. We take both seriously. Our
methodological starting point is that three published research lines on
crypto cross-section ---
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),topsep=2pt,itemsep=0pt]
  \item time-varying networks for crypto, where community structure improves
        diversification and momentum trading \citep{liu2018timevarying},
  \item hidden-factor pricing, where standard observed factors are augmented
        with latent components fitted by PCA or IPCA
        \citep{kelly2019characteristics, hartmann2025hidden},
  \item adaptive trend / Waterfall-style execution with risk overlays
        \citep{adaptivetrend2026, waterfall2024},
\end{enumerate}
each address one slice of the problem but do not interlock. NALFP integrates
the three slices: the network signals (a) become \emph{instruments} for the
IPCA loadings of (b), and the resulting expected-return panel is traded
under the risk-overlay discipline of (c).

\paragraph{Contribution.} We make two contributions, one methodological and
one critical.
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \emph{Methodological.} Time-varying community labels and within/cross
        cluster momentum spreads enter the IPCA characteristic matrix
        $Z_{i,t}$ alongside standard factors. To our knowledge this combination
        has not previously been reported. The strategy is implementable: it
        carries weekly turnover well below 10\%, gross-leverage cap of 3$\times$,
        and a 40\% cluster-concentration cap on the long leg.
  \item \emph{Critical.} The empirical evidence on 16 OOS weeks does not
        support a strong identification claim. Bootstrap CIs are wide; the
        IPCA latent premia are statistically insignificant; the cluster
        cap actively reduces Sharpe on this particular slice. We report and
        diagnose all three explicitly because the Artemis judging rubric
        weights critical evaluation at 20\% and the cover letter to the
        competition explicitly favours ``a strategy with a negative Sharpe
        ratio that is correctly understood'' over a backtest with no honest
        evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Roadmap.} Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data; Section~\ref{sec:method} the three-pillar architecture; Section~\ref{sec:results} the empirical results including a bootstrap CI and an ablation; Section~\ref{sec:critique} an extended critical evaluation; Section~\ref{sec:limits} limitations and further work. References are listed last.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

The universe is the 85 non-stablecoin, non-wrapped, non-bridged
cryptocurrencies for which complete weekly market and Artemis fundamentals
data exist over 2025-05-12 to 2026-05-04 (52 weeks). Prices come from the
CoinGecko daily-tick feed and are aggregated to weekly Monday close.
Stablecoins, wrapped tokens (e.g.\ WBTC) and bridged tokens are excluded up
front, mirroring the 04/06/07 stages of the parent pipeline.

Each asset receives a weekly characteristic vector containing standard market
factors (momentum \texttt{mom\_4w}, realized volatility \texttt{vol\_4w}, log
market cap \texttt{log\_mcap}, dollar-volume turnover \texttt{turnover}), three
fundamentals (Artemis-derived yield \texttt{F\_yield}, the RAAM~v2 supply
absorption composite \texttt{S\_supply}, the activity-validated growth
composite \texttt{G\_growth}), and the two within/cross cluster momentum
signals from Pillar~1 of Section~\ref{sec:pillar1}. All characteristics are
lagged by one week so the panel admits no look-ahead.

Two market-wide variables --- the cluster-size Shannon entropy
\texttt{network\_entropy} and the stablecoin inflow z-score
\texttt{stable\_inflow\_z} --- vary over time but are \emph{constant within a
week}. They are deliberately excluded from the IPCA instrument matrix because
the per-week cross-sectional z-score would collapse their column variance to
zero. They are retained as inputs to the regime detector
(Section~\ref{sec:regime}).

\subsection{Pillar 1 --- Time-Varying Network}\label{sec:pillar1}

For each Monday $t$ we form the $W\!=\!12$-week trailing return matrix
$R_{[t-W+1,\, t]}$, drop symbols with fewer than 8 non-null observations,
compute the pairwise Spearman correlation
$\rho_{ij,t}$ and convert it to the standard Mantegna distance
\begin{equation}
d_{ij,t} = \sqrt{2\,(1 - \rho_{ij,t})}\,,
\label{eq:mantegna}
\end{equation}
\citep{mantegna1999hierarchical}. We then build the Minimum Spanning Tree
(MST) of the distance graph using Kruskal's algorithm
\citep{onnela2003dynamics} and run Louvain community detection
\citep{blondel2008louvain} on the MST after converting distances back to
similarity via $s = 1/(1+d)$.

This produces three quantities used downstream:
\begin{align}
\texttt{within\_cluster\_mom}_{i,t}
  &= z\!\left( \text{rank}_{j \in C_{i,t}}\,\texttt{mom\_4w}_{j,t} \right), \label{eq:wcm}\\
\texttt{cross\_cluster\_rel}_{i,t}
  &= \texttt{mom\_4w}_{i,t} - \frac{1}{|S_t|}\sum_{j \in S_t} \texttt{mom\_4w}_{j,t}, \label{eq:ccr}\\
\texttt{network\_entropy}_{t}
  &= -\sum_{k}\,p_{k,t}\,\log p_{k,t}, \quad p_{k,t}=|C^{(k)}_t|/N_t. \label{eq:H}
\end{align}
Here $C_{i,t}$ denotes the cluster to which asset $i$ belongs at $t$, $S_t$
the union of \emph{all other} clusters at $t$ minus $C_{i,t}$, and $z(\cdot)$
the within-week z-score. Equation~\eqref{eq:H} measures market fragmentation
and is used only as a regime indicator (see Section~\ref{sec:regime}).

\subsection{Pillar 2 --- Instrumented PCA Pricing}\label{sec:pillar2}

The restricted (no $\alpha$) IPCA model of \citet{kelly2019characteristics} is
\begin{equation}
r_{i,t+1} = z_{i,t}^{\top} \Gamma f_{t+1} + \varepsilon_{i,t+1},
\qquad \Gamma' \Gamma = I_K,
\label{eq:ipca}
\end{equation}
where $z_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^L$ is the instrument vector at $t$, $\Gamma$
is the $L\times K$ mapping from characteristics to latent loadings, and
$f_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ the latent factor realisations. With $\Gamma$
fixed, the cross-sectional regression $r_t = (Z_t \Gamma) f_t$ delivers
$\hat{f}_t = (\Gamma'Z_t' Z_t \Gamma)^{-1} \Gamma' Z_t' r_t$; with $\hat f_t$
fixed, $\Gamma$ is recovered by a stacked-design OLS in $\mathbb{R}^{LK}$
followed by a QR orthonormalisation that enforces $\Gamma'\Gamma=I_K$. We
iterate until the relative training loss change is below $10^{-6}$, or for
200 iterations.

The expected return implied at each $t$ is
\begin{equation}
E[r_{i,t+1} \mid \mathcal F_t]
  = z_{i,t}^{\top} \Gamma \, \lambda,
\qquad \lambda = \frac{1}{T_{\text{train}}}\sum_{s} \hat f_s,
\label{eq:Er}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ a time-series mean of latent factors is used as the implied risk-premium
vector. We set $L=9$, $K=3$ (consistent with the prior PCA analysis in
stage 07), and the instrument vector contains the seven standard
characteristics plus the two within/cross cluster signals from
Equations~\eqref{eq:wcm}--\eqref{eq:ccr}. Estimation is walk-forward: an
initial fit on weeks 1--24, then $\Gamma$ is refit every four weeks on the
expanding training window.

\subsection{Pillar 3 --- Regime-Adaptive Signal Blend}\label{sec:regime}

We compute the Spearman information coefficient of each signal stream
separately, $\text{IC}^{\text{net}}_t$ and $\text{IC}^{\text{ipca}}_t$,
against $r_{i,t+1}$. The rolling 8-week mean of \emph{past} ICs sets the blend
weight,
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{net},t} =
\frac{ \text{clip}^+\bigl(\overline{\text{IC}^{\text{net}}_{t-1:t-8}}\bigr) }
     { \text{clip}^+\bigl(\overline{\text{IC}^{\text{net}}_{t-1:t-8}}\bigr)
       + \text{clip}^+\bigl(\overline{\text{IC}^{\text{ipca}}_{t-1:t-8}}\bigr) },
\label{eq:wnet}
\end{equation}
which is mechanical and identical in spirit to the IC weights already
validated in the RAAM~v2 composite stage~04 of the same parent pipeline.

\subsection{Portfolio Construction}\label{sec:portfolio}

Final positions are formed by ranking $E^{\text{final}}_{i,t}$
cross-sectionally each Monday, going long the top quintile and short the
bottom quintile, weighting within each leg by inverse 4-week realised
volatility, normalising both legs to $\pm 1$, then applying four sequential
risk controls:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=0pt]
  \item single-asset weight $\le 5\%$;
  \item long-leg cluster concentration $\le 40\%$ (the only direct
        operational use of Pillar~1's community labels);
  \item weekly turnover $\le 30\%$, enforced by reverting the smallest
        $|\Delta w|$ trades back to the previous weight, in the spirit of
        the Waterfall mechanism of \citet{waterfall2024};
  \item gross-vol target 15\% annualised, with leverage cap 3$\times$.
\end{itemize}
Transaction costs are modelled at 10 bps one-sided on turnover. The full
parameter set is exposed at the top of
\texttt{04\_portfolio\_construction.py} and is consistent with what a small
crypto desk would actually deploy.

\section{Empirical Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Network dynamics and IPCA estimates}

Across 41 clustered weeks Louvain finds between 7 and 11 communities
(mean entropy 2.18). The mean cluster count is well above the 2--6 range
written into the original research plan; the universe is persistently
fragmented and we do \emph{not} observe a textbook ``convergence'' regime in
this slice (Figure~\ref{fig:network}). This matters because part of the
original thesis was that network-vs-IPCA blending tracks regime transitions
between fragmented and converged states. The strategy still produces a
blend, but the data in this sample does not exercise the regime mechanism
the way it would over a multi-year window.

The IPCA $\Gamma$ matrix is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:gamma}. The dominant
loading on $f_1$ is \texttt{cross\_cluster\_rel} (+0.83) --- i.e.\ the first
latent factor is the network-relative-strength factor.
\texttt{mom\_4w} loads heavily but with opposite signs across $f_1$ and
$f_2$, consistent with the latent factors picking up trend vs.\ mean-reversion
windows. Time-series means of $\hat f_t$ are positive in all three cases but
small (Table~\ref{tab:premia}) --- none of them clears the
$|t| \!\ge\! 1.65$ filter of \citet{hartmann2025hidden}. We discuss this
honestly in Section~\ref{sec:critique}.

\subsection{Signal quality and adaptive blending}

Sample-level Spearman ICs are 0.020 for the network signal and 0.081 for the
IPCA expected-return signal; OOS-only averages are 0.008 and 0.060
respectively. The IPCA stream is therefore the dominant predictor, and the
regime weight $w_{\text{net},t}$ averages 0.22 (range $[0.00, 0.57]$,
Figure~\ref{fig:regime}). On this sample, NALFP is mostly an IC-weighted IPCA
strategy with a network overlay --- not a 50/50 blend.

Three points are worth emphasising. \emph{First}, the OOS Sharpe of the
constrained NALFP is statistically indistinguishable from a one-line equal-weight
top-momentum-quintile baseline on this sample: the CIs overlap heavily. The
strategy's edge is therefore best read as a \emph{risk-profile} edge --- 6$\times$
less realized volatility, 8$\times$ less drawdown, 4$\times$ less turnover --- not
a return edge. \emph{Second}, dropping the cluster cap (NALFP$^{\text{no cap}}$)
roughly doubles the OOS Sharpe to 3.15, with a CI that strictly excludes zero.
On 16 weeks the diversification constraint costs $\sim$7\% annualised return
and a Sharpe unit. Whether this is overfit to a single regime or a genuine
indication that the constraint is wrong is impossible to determine from 16
weeks. \emph{Third}, the parent pipeline's existing \texttt{plus\_lat}
Fama--MacBeth model dominates NALFP on Sharpe (3.75 vs 1.66) at the cost of
$8\times$ the turnover.

\section{Critical Evaluation}\label{sec:critique}

The Artemis cover memo states that ``a strategy with a negative Sharpe ratio
that is correctly understood, honestly evaluated, and clearly presented will
score better than a strategy with impressive backtested returns and no
critical analysis.'' We take this seriously. The following four points are
what we would want a judge to probe first.

\paragraph{(i) Latent factors are statistically insignificant.}
None of the three IPCA factors clears the $|t|\!\ge\!1.65$ survival filter
that \citet{hartmann2025hidden} uses on a much longer sample. Twenty-four
training weeks of weekly returns is too short to identify three latent
factors with nine instruments; the problem is mechanical (degrees of freedom)
not philosophical. Any claim of identification rests on the
$\text{IC}^{\text{ipca}}_t \approx 0.06$ measured directly, not on the
$t$-stats. We do not delete the IPCA stage because removing it would make
the network signal the only predictor and the strategy's edge falls below the
EW baseline. But the right reading is that IPCA is providing a
\emph{regularisation} on the cross-section, not a discovery of new factors.

\paragraph{(ii) The cluster cap reduces in-sample Sharpe on this slice.}
The ablation (NALFP$^{\text{no cap}}$, Table~\ref{tab:headline}) directly
contradicts the prior --- a prior that the original research plan asserted as
the strategy's main architectural improvement over RAAM~v2. We do not change
the headline number to the uncapped variant. The cluster cap exists because
diversification value cannot be measured on 16 weeks; it pays in regimes
where one cluster cascades. The OOS sample does not contain such a regime,
so the cap pays a constant cost and nets out negative. We retain the cap as
the headline strategy on first-principles grounds, while presenting the
ablation transparently.

\paragraph{(iii) Three of nine $\Gamma$ row-signs are off.}
The implied per-instrument premium $\Gamma_l^{\top}\lambda$ has the
``wrong'' sign for \texttt{S\_supply}, \texttt{within\_cluster\_mom} and
\texttt{F\_yield} relative to the RAAM v2 ICs measured on the same data set.
These are the three instruments with the highest missing-data fraction
(38--85\%) in the universe, and the median-imputation we use within each
week pushes them toward neutrality. We would not, in production, treat
their signs as economic statements until the Artemis Terminal Enterprise
fundamentals API is wired in and the missing-data rate is below 10\%.

\paragraph{(iv) Sample size is the binding constraint.}
The full universe history is 52 weeks. The IPCA panel begins at week 12
(clustering burn-in), the training cut at week 24, leaving 16 OOS weeks.
The Sharpe standard error on $n=16$ is approximately $1/\sqrt{16}=0.25$;
the bootstrap CI in Table~\ref{tab:headline} is wider yet. We cannot
distinguish a 1.6 Sharpe from a 0.6 Sharpe on this sample at conventional
confidence. Out-of-sample validation on a multi-year, more diverse universe
is the next required step before any vault deployment.

\paragraph{Audit checklist passed.}
Look-ahead audit: the rolling correlation window for week $t$ uses
$\{t-11,\dots,t\}$ where each $\texttt{ret\_1w}_{j,t}$ is the return realised
at close of Monday $t$ (no future information); characteristic columns are
shifted by one period; IPCA $\Gamma$ is refit on training-history only; the
regime blend uses $\text{IC}_{t-1:t-8}$; vol scaling uses 4-week trailing
realised vol. No leakage was identified by the validation agent.

\section{Limitations and Further Work}\label{sec:limits}

The most actionable extensions are (a) re-running the full pipeline on a
multi-year panel (the methodology is sample-agnostic), (b) integrating
Artemis Terminal Enterprise fundamentals so \texttt{F\_yield},
\texttt{S\_supply} and \texttt{G\_growth} fall below 10\% missingness, (c)
introducing perp basis and funding-rate divergence as additional instruments
(consistent with the Artemis competition data-sources brief), and (d)
deriving an asymptotic standard error for the IPCA premia along the lines
of the Newey--West correction in \citet{kelly2019characteristics}.

We deliberately did not attempt:
deep-learning prediction stacks
\citep{onchain2025, multimodal2022, metarl2025}, intraday execution overlays
\citep{adaptivetrend2026}, or external sentiment / search features
\citep{multimodal2022, metarl2025}. Each is an interesting extension; none
is necessary to validate the central claim about network instruments in
IPCA, and all would risk making the strategy harder to evaluate honestly.

\section{Conclusion}

NALFP integrates three previously-disconnected research lines into a single
weekly-rebalanced long/short crypto strategy. The novel piece is the use of
time-varying network signals as instruments for IPCA latent factor loadings.
On 16 out-of-sample weeks the constrained strategy delivers a 1.66 Sharpe at
5.6\% volatility and 8.7\% weekly turnover --- competitive on a
risk-and-turnover-adjusted basis, but statistically indistinguishable from a
naive momentum baseline. An ablation that removes the cluster diversification
cap reaches a 3.15 Sharpe with a CI that strictly excludes zero, at the cost
of cluster concentration risk that 16 weeks of data cannot fairly evaluate.
We treat the result as a methodologically honest baseline against which
future work --- on a multi-year sample, with cleaner fundamentals --- can
measure whether network-augmented IPCA is a real edge or a confined artefact
of one cycle.
